How AIRGUARD works

Think of it as a guard who counts and describes groups of Wi-Fi frames, then checks whether the pattern resembles a known attack.

Show and save the alert

When the score reaches the threshold, the event appears here and is appended to alerts.jsonl. It never blocks a device.

What is deauthentication?

A Wi-Fi message that ends a connection. A flood sends unusually many such messages.

What is RSSI?

A rough measure of how strong a radio signal looks to the receiver.

Why use windows?

One frame alone tells little. Counting a group helps spot a pattern.

Model & results

The classifier

Random Forest from scikit-learn

supervised learning
1

Learn from labeled examples

AWID3 windows are labeled normal or deauthentication.

2

Train many decision trees

Trees learn different feature patterns; the forest combines their votes.

3

Choose the alert cutoff

A validation portion selects a score threshold. Higher score means more attack-like.

Data and limits

How to interpret these scores.

Input features in plain English

The model checks each 100-frame window.

1

Average frame size

Normalized length of frames.

2

Signal average and variation

Normalized RSSI level and spread.

3

Management-frame share

How much of the window is management traffic.

4

Deauthentication count

Total per 100 frames and largest count in a 10-frame run.

5

Retry and protected shares

Fractions of retry-marked and protected frames.

6

Subtype variety

Number of different frame subtypes.

7

Frame-type entropy

A measure of how mixed the frame types are.
